    Read this first

    Five rules before you touch anything.

    Appliances combine mains electricity, water, heat and sometimes gas. These habits keep a simple fix from becoming a dangerous one.

    1. Cut the powerUnplug it. If it's hard-wired (many dishwashers and ovens), switch it off at the breaker — not just the on/off button.
    2. Shut off water and gasClose the water taps behind washers and dishwashers. Never work on a gas connection yourself.
    3. Let it cool downHeating elements in dishwashers, dryers and ovens stay hot long after a cycle ends.
    4. Find your model numberIt tells you the right manual, error codes and replacement parts. Look on a label inside the door or on the back.
    5. Photograph as you goTake a photo before you remove anything so you can put it back exactly as it was.

    Never DIY these

    Call a qualified technician for:

    • Inside a microwave's casingThe high-voltage capacitor can hold a lethal charge even when unplugged.
    • Refrigerant or the sealed systemCompressor, gas leaks and recharging require certified equipment and training.
    • Gas appliances and connectionsGas cookers, dryers and boilers must be serviced by a registered engineer.
    • Mains wiring and control boardsAnything beyond replacing a plug fuse or cleaning a filter.
    • Appliances under warrantyOpening the casing can void it — contact the manufacturer first.

    FAQ

    Good questions.

    Can't find your problem? Check your manual's troubleshooting page — it's usually the fastest route to an answer.

    Is it safe to repair appliances myself?

    Yes, for the kind of fixes in this guide: cleaning filters, clearing hoses, checking seals and settings — with the appliance unplugged. Anything involving internal wiring, refrigerant, gas or the inside of a microwave is not a DIY job.

    The part on my appliance looks different from the diagram.

    Our diagrams show the most common layout. Positions vary by brand — a drain filter might be bottom left instead of bottom right, for example. Your manual has a labelled drawing of your exact model.

    Where do I find my model number?

    On washers and dishwashers it's usually on a label around the door opening or door edge. Fridges have it on an inside side wall, dryers inside the door opening, and microwaves on the back or inside the door frame.

    What do the error codes on my display mean?

    Codes are different for every brand and model, so the same code can mean different things. Search the manufacturer's website for your exact model number — they publish a code list in the manual.

    Will fixing it myself void the warranty?

    Routine maintenance described in your manual (like cleaning the drain filter) won't. Opening the casing or fitting non-genuine parts usually will. If it's still under warranty, contact the manufacturer first.

    Should I repair or replace my appliance?

    A common rule of thumb: if the repair costs more than half the price of a new one and the appliance is well into its expected lifespan (often around 10 years), replacing it usually makes more sense — especially as newer models use less energy.
